# Transferring the Emergence Profile by Scanning the Provisional Crown

Caption: Once the provisional crown has shaped the gingiva, how do we get that shape to the laboratory in a digital impression? Three scans, two ways to capture the inside of the gingiva, and the E-merge protocol, which leaves the scan body out.

## Body

Over a few weeks, the provisional crown has shaped the gingiva around itself. When we remove the provisional crown, the gingiva may start to collapse [1, 3], and a narrow scan body does not prevent this collapse either. So if we scan only the scan body, the laboratory sees the collapsed gingiva, and the definitive crown will not have the shape we built with the provisional crown.

In conventional impressions we solve this problem with a customized impression coping. We screw the provisional crown onto an analog and push it into silicone so that the imprint of its transmucosal part stays in the silicone. Then we screw the impression coping onto the same analog and fill the space between the coping and the silicone with acrylic resin or flowable composite, so that the coping takes on the same transmucosal form as the provisional crown [1]. The details of this method are covered in part five of the implant impression series.

In the digital method the idea is the same: the shape of the gingiva is on the provisional crown itself. The part of the provisional crown that sits inside the gingiva (the transmucosal part) has exactly the shape the gingiva has taken. Only this time, instead of copying this shape onto a coping, we scan the provisional crown itself [2].

## [[+:1.]] Steps

We take three scans, and the software superimposes them [2, 3]:

1. A scan with the provisional crown in the mouth. The coronal part of the provisional crown, the gingival margin and the adjacent teeth are captured.

2. Removing the provisional crown and capturing the shape inside the gingiva, with one of the two ways described below [1].

3. Connecting the scan body and scanning it, to capture the implant position.

The software superimposes the scans using their shared parts, such as the adjacent teeth and the coronal part of the provisional crown [2]. That is why these parts must be captured completely in every scan.

## [[+:2.]] Way one: a quick scan of the gingiva

If the gingiva stays in place for about a minute after the provisional crown is removed, we scan the inside of the gingiva right then, in about 10 seconds [1]. This usually happens when the implant is shallow (the platform less than 4 millimeters below the gingival margin), the gingiva is thick and the emergence profile is conical. These numbers are criteria of the method's original protocol [1, 3].

## [[+:3.]] Way two: scanning the provisional crown outside the mouth

If the implant is deep or the gingiva collapses quickly, there is no time to scan the gingiva [1]. Here we screw the provisional crown onto an analog and scan it all around outside the mouth, including the part that was inside the gingiva [1, 3]. The software matches this scan to the first scan using the coronal part of the provisional crown. In this way, the part of the provisional crown that was inside the gingiva lands in its correct place in the file, and the shape of the gingiva is built from it [2]. That is why the provisional crown must not change at all between these two scans.

## [[+:4.]] Without a scan body: the E-merge protocol

The E-merge protocol was introduced by Dada and colleagues for single-tooth immediate implants. In this method the scan body is never connected in the mouth, and the provisional crown is not removed after healing either [4]. The steps are:

1. Before placing the provisional crown in the mouth, we screw it onto a special analog (Straumann RB Repositionable Implant Analog) and scan the two together outside the mouth [4]. The outer surface of this analog has an index that shows the position of the platform and the rotational orientation of the connection in the scan. In other words, here the analog does the scan body's job. The result of the final scan of the crown is a file that has both the complete shape of the provisional crown and the implant position relative to it.

2. We screw the provisional crown into the mouth.

3. After the gingiva has healed, with the provisional crown in place, we take a regular scan of the mouth and superimpose the two files [4]. The coronal part of the provisional crown is in both files, and the two files are matched using it. Because the implant position relative to the provisional crown was known in the first file, the implant position in the mouth is obtained as well.

[[+key]]This method works only if the provisional crown has not changed at all after the first scan.[[+/key]]

E-merge differs from the reverse scan body, although the logic of both is that the implant position is obtained from the prosthesis itself. With the reverse scan body, the scan bodies are connected to the intaglio surface of a full-arch provisional prosthesis, and the prosthesis is scanned outside the mouth so that the position of several implants is captured without an intraoral scan of the scan bodies [5]. E-merge is for single units and immediate implants, and it uses an indexed analog instead of a scan body.

## [[+:5.]] Limitation

The accuracy of implant scanning may be affected by the scanning method and the implant depth [1]. The sources of this article have described the method, but they have not compared its accuracy with the customized coping.
