# Title
Add the composite to the tooth or to the crown?

# Caption
When closing a contact chairside, it is better to add the composite to the surface of the crown's feldspathic porcelain: the bond is slightly better and the preparation is done outside the mouth.

# Article

In [Managing an open proximal contact at crown delivery](https://dentcast.org/insight/insight-69.html) we wrote that if the contact stays open, composite can be added to the adjacent tooth. Now assume the crown's porcelain is feldspathic. To which surface should we add the composite, the tooth or the crown?

In terms of bond strength, both surfaces are acceptable, but the bond to porcelain etched with hydrofluoric acid and silanated is slightly higher than the bond to enamel.

Another advantage of bonding to the crown is that it is outside the mouth before cementation. We do the etching, rinsing, drying and silanation outside the patient's mouth, with no concern about saliva and blood. Bonding to the enamel of the adjacent tooth, however, is done inside the mouth, and its isolation depends on access and the patient's conditions.

So the bond is slightly better, and the preparation outside the mouth is simpler and easier to control.

If in some situation we decide to close the contact chairside, it is better to do it from the side of the crown's feldspathic porcelain.
