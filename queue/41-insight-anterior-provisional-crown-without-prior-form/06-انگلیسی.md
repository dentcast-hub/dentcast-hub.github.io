# Title
Making an Anterior Provisional Crown When the Tooth's Previous Form Is Not Available

# Caption
When nothing of the anterior tooth's previous form is available, a prefabricated shell and a putty impression can be used to build the outer form, and a composite provisional crown can be delivered in the same visit.

# Article

The patient came in with an empty maxillary central incisor space and, after the post and core was bonded, wanted a provisional crown in the same visit. Nothing of the tooth's previous form or of the patient's crown was available to take an impression from, and sending the work to the lab would have meant a gap of a day or two, which was not acceptable to the patient in the anterior region.

The provisional material I use in the office is composite-based. I had also had a number of prefabricated shells for years, but since switching to composite provisional material I had stopped using them, because the two do not bond well to each other. [[+key]]In this patient I did not use the shell as the provisional crown itself; I only used it to help build the outer form.[[+/key]]

I took a central shell from the box and adjusted it on the tooth and the post and core so that its height and margin were roughly similar to those of the adjacent teeth. Then I placed temporary dressing material inside the shell and pressed it onto the tooth so that it would stay in place. I took a putty impression of the whole assembly, and in this way the outer form of the provisional crown was ready.

[[تصویر:01]]

For the inner form, I removed the shell and the dressing and cleaned the tooth. I placed the composite provisional material in the putty impression and seated the impression on the tooth so that the inner surface of the crown would conform to the tooth structure. Finally, I removed the excess, adjusted the crown and delivered it to the patient in the same visit.
