# When the Lower Anterior Teeth Leave No Room for Upper Restorations, What Should We Do?

Caption: The lower incisal edges leave no room for upper restorations; when is incisal reduction part of the treatment plan, and when does the problem lie in the OVD, the jaw relationship or compensatory eruption?

Suppose we want to restore the maxillary anterior teeth, but the incisal edges of the mandibular anterior teeth leave too little space. Can we take a little off the lower incisal edges and solve the problem?

Sometimes yes, but before reducing anything we need to know whether the problem lies in the lower teeth themselves or in the jaw relationship and posterior support. A review article and a clinical case report help us tell these two situations apart.

## First, Understand Why Anterior Space Has Been Lost

In its discussion of deep bite, the review Pre-prosthetic orthodontics suggests measuring the lower incisal edges against a reference occlusal plane. This matters because the patient's current occlusal plane may have changed with the loss of posterior support.

By the criterion quoted from Kokich, the reference line on a cephalometric radiograph is drawn from the contact of the second molars posteriorly to the level of the upper lip at rest anteriorly. The incisal edges of the mandibular anterior teeth are normally close to this line. If they sit above it, the teeth may have over-erupted (supraeruption).

[[Image:01]]

*Figure 1. The red line is the reference occlusal plane: from the occlusal surfaces of the second molars to the upper lip at rest. Source: Alfallaj 2020, Fig. 1*

If a tooth is worn, do not look only at its edge; the gingival margin and the position of the CEJ also matter. If the tooth is worn and the CEJ lies close to the gingival margin, we should think of compensatory eruption. But when the CEJ is clearly apical to the margin, altered passive eruption also comes into consideration. This distinction helps in choosing treatment, but on its own it is not enough for a diagnosis.

[[Image:02]]

*Figure 2. Worn anterior teeth with short clinical crowns and irregular gingival margins, while the posterior teeth have normal height. Source: Alfallaj 2020, Fig. 2*

In compensatory eruption, shortening the incisal edge does not return the tooth and the gingiva to the right position.

## In the Case Report, Why Were the Lower Anterior Teeth Shortened?

The patient had a skeletal Class III relationship and had long since lost mandibular posterior support. The remaining teeth were severely worn, the occlusal vertical dimension (OVD) had decreased, and there was a severe anterior crossbite.

[[Image:03]]

*Figure 3. The patient's initial condition: severe anterior crossbite, severe tooth wear and loss of posterior support. Source: Koo et al. 2026, Fig. 1*

The clinicians first corrected the OVD and the jaw relationship; to test the new height they made a provisional overlay denture and evaluated the patient over a period of several months. The decision to reduce the incisal edges was therefore made after the new jaw relationship had been tested, not at the start of treatment.

At the new height, the mandible in centric relation sat further back than in its initial position. Along the closing path, the remaining mandibular left teeth made premature contact with the maxillary anterior teeth. The clinicians selectively adjusted the incisal edges of those teeth, with reference to the lower lip and the occlusal plane, until they matched the height of the mandibular right anterior teeth.

After the incisal adjustment, the maxillary anterior teeth were prepared and provisional restorations were made. Provisional posterior support was then established, and at later stages, after a new diagnostic wax-up, the implants were placed and the definitive reconstruction was completed.

[[Image:04]]

*Figure 4. Provisional stages: an overlay denture to test the new height (A and B), the maxillary anterior provisional after the lower incisal adjustment (C) and the full-arch provisional (D). Source: Koo et al. 2026, Fig. 10*

An important point: in this case, supraeruption of the lower anterior teeth had not been diagnosed. These teeth were even shorter than the reference dimensions, and worn. The incisal reduction was done solely to remove an interference that became apparent after the jaw relationship was changed.

## Why Did the Crossbite Improve?

The improvement in the anterior relationship came mainly from restoring the OVD and the backward and downward rotation of the mandible in its new position, not from shortening the teeth. The cephalometric changes also showed this shift, but they did not mean the skeletal Class III discrepancy had been resolved.

[[Image:05]]

*Figure 5. Initial cephalogram (A), with the provisional prosthesis (B) and their superimposition (C), showing the backward and downward rotation of the mandible. Source: Koo et al. 2026, Fig. 11*

In the end, the anterior relationship went from a severe crossbite to edge to edge. This was a compromise treatment: the authors explain that orthodontics or orthognathic surgery would have been needed to achieve normal overjet and overbite, but given the patient's systemic conditions, the cost and length of treatment, and the patient's satisfaction with the result, no further treatment was done. At the reported follow-up, no fracture or debonding of the prosthesis and no new temporomandibular joint problem was observed.

[[Image:06]]

*Figure 6. Definitive restorations with an edge-to-edge anterior relationship. Source: Koo et al. 2026, Fig. 17*

## When Is Orthodontics the Better Choice?

If the anterior teeth have undergone compensatory eruption together with the bone and gingiva, orthodontic intrusion can correct the position of the teeth and the gingival margin and reduce the need for incisal reduction. When planning intrusion, the gingival margin relative to the adjacent teeth is a more important reference than the worn incisal edge.

[[Image:07]]

*Figure 7. Intrusion of the worn anterior teeth (B), using the gingival margins of the adjacent teeth as the reference, followed by restoration (C). Source: Alfallaj 2020, Fig. 3*

Intrusion has its limits too: it takes time, it needs patient cooperation and a post-treatment stabilization period, and it can be accompanied by apical root resorption. So the condition of the roots and the crown-to-root ratio must also be considered in the treatment plan.

In a suitable patient, other options can also be considered: posterior reconstruction and an increase in OVD to create anterior space, surgical crown lengthening in selected cases, or the Dahl concept. The choice of approach should rest on the diagnosis and the wax-up, not only on where two teeth collide.

## What Should We Check Chairside Before Deciding?

Before adjusting the incisal edges of the mandibular anterior teeth, check these: Are the posterior support, the OVD and the jaw relationship correct? Where is the incisal edge relative to the reference occlusal plane? And is there wear, compensatory eruption or a change in the gingival margin?

If, after an appropriate vertical dimension and jaw relationship have been established, only a local interference remains, selective incisal adjustment may be part of the treatment plan. But if the lack of space is caused by compensatory eruption or collapse of posterior support, shortening the tooth alone does not solve the underlying problem.

Limits of the evidence: these articles do not set a specific amount of safe incisal reduction, and they do not show that this adjustment routinely replaces orthodontics.
