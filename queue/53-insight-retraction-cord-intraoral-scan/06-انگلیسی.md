# Title
Is a Retraction Cord Still Needed for an Intraoral Scan?

# Caption
A scanner records only the surface it can see; wherever the finish line is subgingival, the margin is not captured reliably in the scan without a retraction cord and a dry field.

# Article

**Question**

When I scan instead of taking an impression, do I still need to place a retraction cord?

**Answer**

A scanner records only the surface it can see. Impression material can enter the sulcus to some extent and push the gingiva aside, but the scanner's light cannot do that. For this reason, displacing the gingiva matters even more in scanning, and it cannot be left out.

[[+]] ### ∆ 1. Supragingival or subgingival finish line

[[Allowed]] If the finish line is supragingival or at the level of the gingiva and the field stays dry, an acceptable scan can usually be obtained without a cord. The problem starts when the finish line lies subgingivally. In that situation the gingiva falls over the margin, and the software records the preparation boundary more coronally than its true position. The result is a crown made shorter than the real margin. Laboratory studies show that this error exceeds the acceptable limit from a depth of about half a millimeter below the gingiva onward, and that it is resolved by cord placement.

[[+]] ### ∆ 2. Where the cord sits, and scanning with it in place

The cord should sit below the finish line. [[Allowed]] When the cord is in this position, there is no need to remove it for the scan, and the scan can be taken with the cord in place. Removing the cord starts bleeding in many cases, and this bleeding covers the margin.

> [[+]] **Clinical tip**
>
> The only thing that must be checked before scanning is that no part of the cord or its fibers has come up over the finish line, because the scanner records it as part of the margin.

[[+]] ### ∆ 3. Cord placement area by area

Cord placement is not needed all the way around the tooth either, and the decision has to be made area by area. A cord only works where there is a retractable sulcus ([Crown Replacement and Soft Tissue Management with Inflammation from Food Impaction](/insight/en/insight-73.html)). In a tooth where one wall is amalgam close to the gingiva, the sulcus on that side is not deep enough. A cord there causes bleeding, does not stay in place, and falls onto that same area during the scan. That is why that side is skipped and the boundary is left to a vertical finish line ([Why Don't We Need 360-Degree Cord Packing on Teeth with Amalgam Restorations?](/insight/en/insight-67.html)).

[[+]] ### ∆ 4. A dry field

Another condition is a dry field. Saliva and blood reduce the accuracy of margin capture, and this effect is stronger for a subgingival margin ([A Dry Field for Scanning: The Role of the Cotton Roll in Removing Moisture, Not Just Saliva Control](/insight/en/insight-74.html)).

[[+]] ### ∆ 5. The cost of the cord to the tissue

Cord placement is not without cost to the tissue, but this cost is usually small. Gingival recession after cord placement is mild in most cases and depends on the pressure used while placing the cord and on how long it stays in. [[Caution]] If the cord is impregnated with aluminum chloride, it is better not to leave it in the sulcus for more than about ten minutes.

> [[+]] **Bottom line**
>
> Overall, wherever the finish line is subgingival, a scan without displacing the gingiva is not reliable. If the margin still does not show clearly in the scan despite the cord and moisture control, a conventional impression is the safer choice.
