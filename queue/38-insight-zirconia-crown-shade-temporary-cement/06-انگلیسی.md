# Title
Zirconia Crown Shade on Temporary Cement: Why Doesn't the Crown Match the Laminates?

# Caption
White, chalky temporary cement can make a zirconia crown look lighter and more opaque; trying the crown in without cement shows whether the shade difference comes from the restoration itself or from the cement layer beneath it.

# Article

[[تصویر:01]]

In one case, the two upper laterals were laminates and the rest of the maxillary restorations were zirconia crowns. I cemented the laminates with resin cement, but delivered the crowns on temporary cement.

The patient came back and said their teeth had become two-toned and the shade of the laminates did not match the rest of the crowns. I told them they were right, and that the work is not finished until they themselves are satisfied with the result. But I explained that this shade difference most likely comes from the temporary cement.

Temporary cement is white and chalky and highly opaque. Zirconia, too, despite being more opaque than glass ceramics, lets some light pass through it. So the layer of temporary cement under the crown can affect its final shade and make the crown look lighter and more opaque.

On the other hand, the laminates had been cemented with resin cement and were showing their final shade. In effect, we had two kinds of restoration side by side: one showing its own final shade, and the other affected by the color of the temporary cement.

To show this to the patient, I removed the upper left central crown, cleaned it and placed it on the tooth without cement. This time its shade matched the laminates, and the patient confirmed the match too.

This is the same problem I wrote about earlier regarding a crown's shade changing after permanent cementation; only this time we are looking at it from the other side. In that case, the crown's shade was right on temporary cement but changed after permanent cementation. Here, conversely, the temporary cement itself had changed the crown's apparent shade.

What the two cases have in common is that the cement is part of the restoration's optical system and can affect its final shade.

So when a patient is unhappy with the shade of a crown delivered on temporary cement, we should not take the crown's shade under those conditions as its final shade. Trying the crown in without cement is a simple way to find out whether the shade difference comes from the restoration itself or from the cement layer beneath it.
