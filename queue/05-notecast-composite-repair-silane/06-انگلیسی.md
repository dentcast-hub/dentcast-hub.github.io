## Title
Repairing Old Composite: Silane and Not Curing the Bonding Agent

## Caption
A DentCast NoteCast based on the study by Eliasson and Dahl (2017); the effect of silane and of not curing the bonding agent on the bond strength of new composite to old composite, and the proposed repair protocol.

## Body
One of the clinical challenges is bonding new composite to old composite. This episode reviews a laboratory study that looked for the protocol with the highest bond strength in these restorations.

**What the study did**
The composites were aged with thermocycling. Two things were then compared: with or without silane, and a bonding agent that was cured or not cured before the new composite was placed.

**Silane**
Silane significantly increased the bond strength between the old and the new composite and should not be left out of the repair protocol.

**Not curing the bonding agent**
Not curing the bonding agent before placing the new composite gave higher strength. Here the bonding agent acts like a wetting agent and fills the microscopic irregularities of the surface. This effect was independent of silane and was seen in the groups with silane and in the groups without silane.

**Proposed protocol**
- Roughening the surface of the old composite with a bur
- Applying silane
- Applying the bonding agent without light-curing
- Placing the new composite and final curing

**Summary**
Silane and not curing the bonding agent both increase the strength of the repair of old composite.
