# Increasing OVD: When Is It Necessary and How Much Can We Change It?

Caption: When considering a change in vertical dimension during occlusal rehabilitation, where should we start? A look at treatment-planning decisions in the review by Calamita and colleagues.

## Body

In 2019, Calamita, Coachman, Sesma, and Kois published a paper on a longstanding question in prosthodontics: changing the occlusal vertical dimension (OVD). The paper's main point is that OVD is flexible. The masticatory system can adapt to changes in vertical dimension within a comfort zone.

## Does tooth wear mean OVD has decreased?

Tooth wear alone does not establish a loss of OVD. As teeth wear, compensatory dentoalveolar eruption can maintain vertical dimension. A patient may therefore have severely worn anterior teeth while retaining their OVD.

[[تصویر:01]]

The posterior teeth matter in this assessment. If they remain well positioned and show little wear, an actual reduction in OVD is unlikely. A true reduction is more likely in patients with posterior bite collapse.

[[تصویر:02]]

For the authors, the more important question is whether changing OVD serves the patient's treatment goals. Identifying and restoring a previous vertical dimension is not necessarily the goal.

## When do we increase OVD?

The paper gives three main reasons: improving dentofacial esthetics, creating adequate space for restorative materials, and improving occlusal relationships.

Increasing OVD can create room to restore worn teeth without removing more tooth structure to achieve the required material thickness. Modern materials and bonding techniques make minimally invasive, additive treatment possible for many of these patients and can reduce the need for extensive root canal treatment and posts for retention.

An increase in OVD alone may have little effect on facial appearance, especially in dentate patients.

## What happens to anterior tooth relationships when we increase OVD?

I find this one of the most clinically useful parts of the paper.

As OVD increases, the mandible rotates as it opens. Overbite decreases and overjet increases.

In the geometry the paper describes, opening by approximately 1 mm at the second molar reduces overbite by about 2 mm and increases overjet by about 1.3 mm. The actual relationship depends on the patient's facial morphology and jaw relationship.

[[تصویر:03]]

This can be a problem in Class II patients. Anterior contacts become harder to establish, and achieving contact may require excessive bulk on the palatal surfaces of the maxillary teeth. This can affect speech, patient comfort, and function.

[[تصویر:04]]

In some Class III patients, the same change can help improve the anterior relationship.

[[تصویر:05]]

Increasing OVD therefore does not guarantee a better occlusion.

## Does increasing OVD pose a risk to the temporomandibular joint?

The studies reviewed suggest that appropriately selected patients usually tolerate a controlled change in OVD. One review cited in the paper reported that a permanent increase of up to approximately 5 mm was safe and predictable under suitable conditions.

Patients may initially experience muscle discomfort or mild symptoms, which are often transient. The evidence reviewed does not show that a permanent increase in OVD by itself causes lasting temporomandibular disorders.

The magnitude of the increase is one consideration. The clinician also needs to assess joint status, simultaneous bilateral contacts, the direction of forces on the teeth, mandibular movements, and speech.

[[تصویر:07]]

## How do we determine the new OVD?

The paper emphasizes that facial proportions, freeway space, phonetic tests, and cephalometry each have limitations when used alone.

The authors propose facially driven treatment planning.

First, we establish the three-dimensional position of the maxillary anterior incisal edges based on the face, tooth display at rest, and the smile. Then we assess the position of the mandibular teeth and their functional relationships.

[[تصویر:08]]

Once the new anterior tooth positions are established, we decide whether an increase in OVD is necessary and, if so, how much.

A repeatable jaw relationship such as centric relation or adapted centric posture is important for this assessment. The authors also suggest using a deprogrammer in complex cases.

[[تصویر:09]]

The palatal contours of the maxillary anterior teeth also matter. Lengthening the incisal edges can create an excessively steep or restrictive path for mandibular movement. Once the anterior relationships are established, the clinician completes the design of the posterior occlusion.

[[تصویر:10]]

## What should we do before definitive treatment?

The authors recommend testing the new OVD with a bonded mock-up or fixed provisional restorations. This allows a more representative assessment of esthetics, speech, comfort, and function.

[[تصویر:11]]

A removable splint lacks the stability and contours of natural teeth, making it less suitable for determining whether a patient tolerates the new OVD. It can still be used for deprogramming.

Before proceeding with irreversible treatment, confirm patient comfort, speech, function, and occlusal stability.

[[تصویر:12]]

## Conclusion

OVD can be adjusted within the limits of the patient's physiology and the demands of treatment.

Tooth wear alone does not justify increasing OVD. First assess anterior tooth position, restorative requirements, and functional relationships. Then choose the **smallest increase necessary** to provide sufficient restorative space, improve occlusion, and minimize the adaptation required of the masticatory system.
