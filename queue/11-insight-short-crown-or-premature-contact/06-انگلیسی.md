# Title
Short Crown or Premature Contact?

# Caption
The implant crown is fully seated, but it does not make adequate contact with the opposing tooth.

Should it be sent back to the laboratory to correct its height?

A closer occlusal assessment can sometimes change the treatment plan.

#ClinicalInsight #Occlusion #ImplantCrown #PrematureContact #ImplantOcclusion

# Article

In one patient, I tried in an implant crown on the mandibular left molar. The opposing tooth also had an implant crown.

After confirming complete seating, I noticed that the buccal cusps were separated from the opposing tooth by an estimated few tenths of a millimeter, with no occlusal contact.

At first, the crown appeared slightly short. I decided to take a putty bite registration so I could show the gap to the laboratory and have the crown adjusted.

But the putty registration revealed something else.

[[Image:01]]

*Clinical image: Putty bite registration from this case; contact interpretation was made together with the clinical examination.*

There was a substantially heavier contact around one of the lingual cusps than at the other sites. This raised the possibility that a premature contact was preventing the mandible from closing fully in the intended position, leaving the buccal cusps out of contact.

The discrepancy was subtle enough that I had not noticed it on the initial examination. Because both teeth were implant-supported, reduced sensitivity to subtle contacts may also have made the interference less noticeable to the patient.

I made a limited adjustment to the heavy contact and reassessed the occlusion.

The buccal cusps then made contact, without adding porcelain or returning the crown to the laboratory to increase its height.

## Clinical insight

When a crown is fully seated but lacks contact in part of the occlusal surface, it is not necessarily too short.

Sometimes a premature contact elsewhere prevents the mandible from reaching its final closing position and creates an appearance similar to a short crown.

A putty bite registration can show an occlusal gap and may also provide clues about uneven contacts; however, these findings should be confirmed clinically.

**Before deciding to add to the crown height, it is worth checking whether a premature contact elsewhere explains the gap.**
