## Title
Where Should I Check for Temporary Cement After Removing a Crown?

## Caption
After removing a crown, where do you check for residual cement?

## Body
When I provisionally cement a crown and remove it some time later, before cleaning off the cement, I do one simple thing: **I inspect both the tooth surface and the inner surface of the crown.**

Some cement may remain on the tooth and some inside the crown; it does not all have to remain on one surface. What matters to me is **the overall pattern of residual cement across both surfaces.**

[[Image:01]]

*Figure 1 — After removing the crown, I inspect the cement remaining on the tooth before cleaning it.*

Particularly along the margins, I look for an area where **no cement remains either on the tooth or in the corresponding area inside the crown.** If I find one, I treat it as a cue for further examination.

[[Image:02]]

*Figure 2 — I also inspect the inside of the crown; evaluating only one of the two surfaces is not enough.*

**This observation is not a definitive diagnosis of a marginal gap or incomplete crown seating.** Cement may not have been well distributed in that area from the start, may have worn away during use, or may have detached when the crown was removed. Conversely, the presence of cement alone does not prove that its thickness was appropriate or that the crown was fully seated.

Still, a suspicious area is worth examining more closely. Depending on the situation, proximal contacts, the seating path, and the margin can be reassessed; a **Fit Checker or silicone wash** can be used to assess internal fit, and the recorded film thickness can be measured if needed.

**Clinical note:** I do not use residual cement to *definitively confirm crown fit*; I use it as a clue to find areas that **need further examination**. For this, the tooth and the inner surface of the crown must be considered together.
