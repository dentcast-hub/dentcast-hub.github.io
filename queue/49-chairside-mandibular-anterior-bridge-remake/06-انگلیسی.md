# Title
Patient with a mandibular anterior bridge: the decision to remake it

# Caption
The patient has come back to have a mandibular anterior bridge remade, and their main complaint is esthetics. On examination, the bulky form of the abutment crowns at the incisal edge raises a question about the height of the cores themselves.

# Body
## Patient history and complaint

I had previously placed the posts and cores of the abutment teeth for this patient myself, but the crowns and the bridge were made at another practice. Now the patient has come back, saying they are not satisfied with the current work and want a new bridge made for them. When I asked what exactly bothers them, it became clear that their main complaint is esthetics.

## Examination and diagnosis

When a patient comes in, I usually see the problems of the work and their solutions during the examination. Even so, I also ask the patient what they do not like, to understand their point of view and to compare their expectation with what is possible in practice. For example, if the patient's complaint is the gingival area under the pontic, even if we remake the bridge, we may not be able to fully meet that wish.

On examining this bridge, the form of the crowns and the pontic is not appropriate. The bridge retainers are incomplete, and in some areas the crown margins also continue over the gingiva, like a ridge-lap pontic that sits on the gingiva but is hollow underneath. It appears that the impression was taken without retraction cord and the work was made in the laboratory from that same impression.

Another point I noticed in the examination concerns the cores themselves. At the incisal edge of the abutment crowns, the crown form is bulky and inappropriate. This usually means that the ceramic has been forced out of the natural tooth form to cover a long core. To make sure, I check the ceramic thickness in this area. If the ceramic thickness is normal, the problem lies in the core height, and the core was longer than necessary.

[[تصویر:03]]

## Treatment plan

I use the previous bridge as a guide. Given the finding above, I shorten the cores before the impression so that there is enough space for the ceramic and the technician can make the crowns with the right form.

## Cost considerations

The patient's bill also included the cost of a provisional crown, which I removed. The previous bridge both helped with the diagnosis and, between appointments, keeps the abutment teeth from shifting. So there is no need for a separate provisional crown, and there is no reason to impose an extra cost on the patient.
