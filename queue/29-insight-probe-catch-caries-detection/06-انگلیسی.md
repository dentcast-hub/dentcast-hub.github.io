# Title
The probe caught; does that mean the tooth is carious?

# Caption
A probe catch is a mechanical observation, not a definitive diagnosis of caries; the main diagnosis rests on a careful visual examination of a clean, dry tooth surface.

# Article

One of the traditional criteria for detecting caries, especially in occlusal fissures, is the catching of the probe tip. We insert the probe into the fissure, and if it catches when we withdraw it, we suspect caries. But how reliable is this method, really?

The first problem is the probe itself. Probes are not identical in shape, angle and tip diameter. In addition, the force the dentist applies also varies. It is even possible for one probe to catch in a fissure while another probe does not catch in the same fissure.

More importantly, a probe catch is not necessarily a sign of caries. Some fissures are naturally narrow and deep, and the probe tip can catch in them merely because of the anatomy of the fissure.

In its 2023 position statement on caries risk assessment and prevention, the American College of Prosthodontists explicitly notes that scientific evidence has not shown that a catch of a sharp explorer, compared with visual examination of a dry tooth, increases the sensitivity or specificity of caries detection.

It is not only a matter of diagnostic error. Inserting a sharp probe with heavy pressure may damage the surface of an early, non-cavitated lesion, a lesion that might have been manageable without preparation, by controlling risk factors and remineralization.

## So what is the alternative?

Today, the main method is a careful visual examination of a clean, dry tooth surface. In systems such as ICDAS, the visible changes of the enamel, the clarity of the lesion after drying and the presence or absence of a cavity matter. When needed, we also use bitewing radiography.

We do not have to give up the probe either; but instead of pressing its sharp tip into the fissure, it is better to use a ball-ended probe with gentle pressure to examine the surface characteristics.

## What about Caries Detector?

This solution can help assess dentin during caries removal, but it is not a substitute for the initial examination for diagnosing caries. Moreover, a dentin stain does not necessarily mean the dentin is infected or needs to be removed, and if we decide only on the basis of color, especially in deep caries, there is a risk of over-removing dentin.

In fact, we should separate two issues: diagnosing the presence of caries and deciding how much carious tissue to remove. These two have different criteria.

In short, a probe catch is a mechanical observation, not a definitive diagnosis of caries. If we are going to prepare a tooth on the basis of this finding, we need more reliable evidence for our decision.
