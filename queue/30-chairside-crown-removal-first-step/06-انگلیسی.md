## Title
When removing the crown is the first step in treatment

## Caption
The patient was referred with food trapping and bad breath. The first molar had a crown, and the teeth on either side needed restorations. Where should treatment begin?

## Body
An endodontist referred a patient to me for evaluation. The patient reported that food was getting under the crown on tooth 6 and that their breath smelled bad. Teeth 5 and 7 also needed restorations.

[[تصویر:01]]

I had two questions at this visit: could tooth 6 be retained, and in what order should we treat the teeth?

With the crown in place, I could not directly inspect the underlying tooth or assess the condition of its remaining walls. I made removal of the crown on tooth 6 my first step. Removing it would let me inspect the walls directly and assess the tooth’s prognosis.

Removing the crown had another advantage: it improved access to the proximal surfaces of teeth 5 and 7, making it easier to restore them well.

Once the crown was off, the remaining walls of tooth 6 were acceptable, and I judged that the tooth could be retained. I referred the patient back to the endodontist for root canal treatment with a referral letter. The definitive restoration of tooth 6 is planned after root canal treatment.

Treatment sequence for this patient:
1. Remove the crown on tooth 6 and assess the remaining walls and prognosis.
2. Restore teeth 5 and 7 with improved access.
3. Refer tooth 6 for root canal treatment.
4. Place the definitive restoration on tooth 6.

Clinical point: When the condition of a tooth under an old crown is uncertain, removing the crown can clarify its prognosis and improve access for treating the adjacent teeth.
